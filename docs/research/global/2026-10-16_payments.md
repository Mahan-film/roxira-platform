# Payments and Company Setup for a Malaysian Sdn Bhd Selling SaaS Globally in 2026: Stripe Malaysia vs. Paddle vs. Lemon Squeezy vs. Polar

## Introduction and Scope

A small Malaysian Sdn Bhd selling software-as-a-service (SaaS) globally in 2026 faces two intertwined decisions: who legally sells to the customer, and who carries the indirect-tax liability that follows the customer's jurisdiction. The first decision determines the fee structure; the second determines whether the company must register, collect, and remit value-added tax (VAT), goods and services tax (GST), or sales tax in dozens of jurisdictions. This report evaluates Stripe (including Stripe Managed Payments), Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, and Polar as candidate payment stacks for a Malaysian-incorporated seller, covering fees, KYC and onboarding, payouts to Malaysian banks, restricted-country and sanctions exposure, Malaysian service tax (SST) on digital services, EU VAT treatment under a merchant of record (MoR), and the documents typically required. Where the available sources do not confirm a Malaysia-specific fact, that gap is flagged explicitly rather than assumed.

## 1. The Strategic Filter: Merchant of Record vs. Payment Processor

The single most consequential distinction is whether the provider is a payment processor or a merchant of record. A payment processor such as raw Stripe moves money and leaves the tax paperwork with the seller; an MoR is legally the seller, so it calculates, collects, and remits sales tax and VAT worldwide and owns the relationship with the card networks ([ShipGarden, 2026](https://www.shipgarden.com/gallery/polar-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-paddle-merchant-of-record-nextjs-2026)). A merchant of record is defined as the legal entity that sells a product to the end customer and is responsible for collecting and remitting applicable taxes (VAT, GST, sales tax) in every jurisdiction where customers pay; the obligation exists regardless of the company's country of incorporation ([FintechSpecs, 2026](https://fintechspecs.com/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-polar-merchant-of-record-b2b-saas)).

For a Malaysian Sdn Bhd, this matters because the company's incorporation country does not shield it from consumption-tax obligations in customer jurisdictions. Paddle notes that there is no threshold for non-EU sellers of digital services to EU consumers, no threshold for non-UK businesses selling digital services to UK consumers (registration with HMRC is required from the first sale), and that Saudi Arabia and the UAE typically enforce a zero-threshold for non-resident sellers of B2C digital services ([Paddle, 2026](https://www.paddle.com/blog/saas-sales-tax-state-wide-and-international)). Chile, Mexico, Russia, South Korea, Turkey, and Colombia generally require registration from the first B2C digital sale by a non-resident vendor ([Paddle, 2026](https://www.paddle.com/blog/saas-sales-tax-state-wide-and-international)).

Two caveats must be stated plainly. First, an MoR handles indirect tax only; it does not handle income tax, payroll, or entity filings, and the seller must still account for payouts and assess its own income taxes ([TurboStarter, 2026](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-polar-vs-creem-choosing-the-right-saas-payment-provider)). Second, using an MoR shifts VAT liability entirely to the MoR, which becomes the official seller, but typically at higher fees (around 5%) and with less control over branding and customer data; this is most viable for SaaS sellers with low average contract value selling primarily B2C, and is not usually appropriate for enterprise B2C customers who expect invoices from the actual vendor ([Numeral, n.d.](https://www.numeral.com/blog/global-vat-compliance-for-saas-and-software-companies)).

## 2. Provider-by-Provider Assessment

### 2.1 Stripe (Classic) and Stripe Managed Payments

Stripe is a payment processor, not an MoR: the seller is the merchant of record, and Stripe Tax calculates tax but does not file it ([FintechSpecs, 2026](https://fintechspecs.com/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-polar-merchant-of-record-b2b-saas)). US standard online card pricing is 2.9% + 30¢ per successful domestic-card transaction, with additional costs for Stripe Billing (Starter, +0.5%), Stripe Tax (+0.5%), and international cards (+1.5%) ([FintechSpecs, 2026](https://fintechspecs.com/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-polar-merchant-of-record-b2b-saas); [F3Fundit, 2026](https://f3fundit.com/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-micro-saas-2026)). Stripe Tax is priced at 0.5% per transaction, but the seller must still register in each jurisdiction ([StarterPick, 2026](https://starterpick.com/guides/stripe-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-paddle-2026)).

Stripe Managed Payments adds Stripe as merchant of record for an extra 3.5% on top of standard Stripe fees, and is available in one of its 39 supported countries ([TurboStarter, 2026](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-polar-vs-creem-choosing-the-right-saas-payment-provider)). Stripe currently lists direct or extended-network availability in 50 countries and regions, with India and Indonesia marked Preview ([goilerplate, 2026](https://goilerplate.com/blog/polar-vs-stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy)). **Evidence gap:** none of the provided sources confirm that Malaysia is among Stripe's supported merchant countries or among the 39 countries eligible for Stripe Managed Payments. A Malaysian Sdn Bhd must verify this directly with Stripe before designing its stack around it. The Lemon Squeezy documentation illustrates how country-specific onboarding constraints work in practice: from May 2024, Stripe only accepts new accounts from India on an invite-only basis, meaning merchants can only set up a bank payout method if pre-approved, otherwise they must use PayPal for payouts ([Lemon Squeezy Docs, n.d.](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/supported-countries)).

### 2.2 Paddle

Paddle operates as the merchant of record for software companies: Paddle appears on the customer's statement, issues the invoice, and remits VAT, sales tax, or GST in the applicable jurisdiction, with the seller receiving a payout net of fees and taxes ([FintechSpecs, 2026](https://fintechspecs.com/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-polar-merchant-of-record-b2b-saas)). Paddle lists 5% + 50¢ per Checkout transaction on its pay-as-you-go plan, with tax compliance and payment-related buyer support included; custom pricing is available ([F3Fundit, 2026](https://f3fundit.com/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-micro-saas-2026)). It handles tax across 40+ countries ([FintechSpecs, 2026](https://fintechspecs.com/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-polar-merchant-of-record-b2b-saas)).

Paddle's operational profile is attractive for a Malaysian seller: it pays out in 31 currencies with 5 balance currencies, converts monthly on the 1st and pays by the 15th with a $100 minimum, and its international card fee is included in the 5% headline rate ([GlobalSolo, 2026](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-2026)). It is an independent company with a stable roadmap, and subscription billing, dunning, and revenue recovery are strengths ([CodeCudos, 2026](https://codecudos.com/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-merchant-of-record-2026)). At scale, Paddle's volume discounts make it cheaper than Lemon Squeezy: at $100,000 MRR, Paddle is roughly $4,500/month (~$54,000/year) versus Lemon Squeezy at $5,500/month ($66,000/year) ([StarterPick, 2026](https://starterpick.com/guides/stripe-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-paddle-2026)).

### 2.3 Lemon Squeezy

Lemon Squeezy is a merchant of record for digital products and has been owned by Stripe since July 2024 ([TurboStarter, 2026](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-polar-vs-creem-choosing-the-right-saas-payment-provider)). As of October 2026 it still lists 5% + 50¢ per transaction, with extra fees for international (+1.5%), PayPal (+1.5%), and subscription (+0.5%) payments ([TurboStarter, 2026](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-polar-vs-creem-choosing-the-right-saas-payment-provider)). The percentage fee applies to total order value including tax, and 1% applies for bank payouts outside the US ([F3Fundit, 2026](https://f3fundit.com/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-micro-saas-2026)). Payouts are free to US bank accounts and 1% outside the US ([TurboStarter, 2026](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-polar-vs-creem-choosing-the-right-saas-payment-provider)).

The strategic context is direction of travel. In a January 2026 update, co-founder JR Farr said the team has been building Stripe Managed Payments, acknowledged slower support and fewer Lemon Squeezy updates as a result, and said the goal is to give Lemon Squeezy users an easy way to migrate to Managed Payments ([TurboStarter, 2026](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-polar-vs-creem-choosing-the-right-saas-payment-provider)). By 2026, Lemon Squeezy's own site brands the product "Lemon Squeezy + Stripe Managed Payments," and it remains an MoR at 5% + 50 cents ([ShipGarden, 2026](https://www.shipgarden.com/gallery/polar-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-paddle-merchant-of-record-nextjs-2026)). Net sales are held for 13 days, payouts are created on the 1st and 15th, and can take 1–5 days to reach a bank account ([goilerplate, 2026](https://goilerplate.com/blog/polar-vs-stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy)). Payouts are USD only; bank payouts are convertible and PayPal payouts are always USD ([GlobalSolo, 2026](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-2026)). Lemon Squeezy is a fine choice if a business is already on it or sells digital products with license keys, but for new projects the roadmap points toward Stripe Managed Payments ([TurboStarter, 2026](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-polar-vs-creem-choosing-the-right-saas-payment-provider)).

### 2.4 Polar

Polar is an open-source (Apache-2.0) merchant of record that is independently owned, with an entry fee of 5% + 50¢ ([ShipGarden, 2026](https://www.shipgarden.com/gallery/polar-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-paddle-merchant-of-record-nextjs-2026)). It handles VAT/sales tax for transactions it processes, supports subscriptions and one-time payments, and offers an excellent, modern developer experience with reliable webhooks ([goilerplate, 2026](https://goilerplate.com/blog/polar-vs-stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy); [FintechSpecs, 2026](https://fintechspecs.com/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-polar-merchant-of-record-b2b-saas)). Payouts run 4 to 7 business days after manual withdrawal, and Polar passes through Stripe payout fees ($2 per month of active payouts plus 0.25% + 25¢ per payout) ([goilerplate, 2026](https://goilerplate.com/blog/polar-vs-stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy); [TurboStarter, 2026](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-polar-vs-creem-choosing-the-right-saas-payment-provider)). Polar lists payout support across more than 100 countries and accepts customer payments globally except in sanctioned countries ([goilerplate, 2026](https://goilerplate.com/blog/polar-vs-stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy)).

Polar is recommended as the default for indie SaaS and small teams that want an MoR, and for developers who value open source, transparent pricing, and developer tooling ([goilerplate, 2026](https://goilerplate.com/blog/polar-vs-stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy); [TurboStarter, 2026](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-polar-vs-creem-choosing-the-right-saas-payment-provider)). One caution: Polar is a newer entrant, developer-first, and worth tracking but not yet at the maturity of the three big options ([getstacksmart, 2026](https://getstacksmart.com/blog/stripe-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-paddle-solopreneur)). A separate comparison notes that Polar's current pricing and integration details need checking before a reliable cost comparison can be made, and that older descriptions of GitHub issue pledges should not be relied upon without confirmation ([ProcessorVerdict, 2026](https://processorverdict.com/blog/polar-sh-vs-lemon-squeezy-for-open-source-saas-2026-comparison)).

A rough heuristic: under $50,000/year in digital product sales, an MoR is usually cheaper end-to-end; above $200,000/year, Stripe plus a self-managed tax setup is usually cheaper ([getstacksmart, 2026](https://getstacksmart.com/blog/stripe-vs-lemon-squeezy-vs-paddle-solopreneur)).

## 4. KYC, Onboarding, and Payouts to Malaysian Banks

Onboarding is not instantaneous and should be treated as a launch dependency. Lemon Squeezy advises checking store activation and identity requirements before scheduling launch, and explicitly warns not to assume a ten-minute approval ([F3Fundit, 2026](https://f3fundit.com/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-micro-saas-2026)). Lemon Squeezy can offer services to merchants and affiliates who can receive bank or PayPal payouts in one of the hundreds of countries it supports; PayPal payouts are supported in 200+ countries and regions, while bank payouts are supported across a long published country list ([Lemon Squeezy Docs, n.d.](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/supported-countries)). **Evidence gap:** the published bank-payout list is alphabetical and the available excerpt jumps from Ecuador to Tunisia, so Malaysia's presence on that list cannot be confirmed from the source; a Malaysian Sdn Bhd should verify Malaysia directly and treat PayPal payouts (200+ countries) as the fallback channel.

Payout mechanics differ materially for a Malaysian bank account. Lemon Squeezy pays bank payouts in USD only, so a Malaysian-currency bank account would convert on every payout at the receiving bank's rate, on top of platform fees; a founder banking in USD would not notice ([GlobalSolo, 2026](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-2026)). Paddle pays out in 31 currencies, which is a structural advantage for a Malaysian entity that wants to avoid repeated conversion ([GlobalSolo, 2026](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-2026)). Polar passes through Stripe payout fees and supports payouts across more than 100 countries ([TurboStarter, 2026](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-polar-vs-creem-choosing-the-right-saas-payment-provider); [goilerplate, 2026](https://goilerplate.com/blog/polar-vs-stripe-vs-lemonsqueezy)). Stripe's payouts match the bank country, with multi-currency settlement available ([GlobalSolo, 2026](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-2026)).
